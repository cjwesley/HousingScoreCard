Illinois Area-Matched Affordability Gap

MoneyLion’s 2026 Mortgage Stress Index reports a statewide income surplus of +$20,002 for Illinois homebuyers, computed from statewide medians with principal-and-interest mortgage costs only. This table re-runs their formula area-matched for all 102 Illinois counties on the validated specification (aggregate-based effective property tax rates, 2026-scaled incomes, a June-vintage statewide anchor), then adds each county’s property tax. Click any column header to sort. Oak Park is highlighted; the statewide row is shaded gray.

Sources. Median household income and population: U.S. Census Bureau, ACS 2023 5-year, tables B19013 and B01003, with incomes scaled to 2026 by the 1.0207 factor implied by MoneyLion’s Illinois income versus the ACS statewide figure; the statewide row uses MoneyLion’s published 2026 income ($83,390) and published income-needed ($63,388) verbatim. Home values: Zillow ZHVI, single-family mid-tier, seasonally adjusted, July 2026 (statewide row: June 2026, matching the source study’s data-as-of date); ZHVI is a typical-value index, not a sales median. Effective property tax rates: ACS 2023 5-year aggregate real estate taxes paid (B25090) divided by aggregate value of owner-occupied homes (B25082) per county, a construct that avoids the $10,000 top-code on ACS median tax figures and is computed identically for every row; these are incumbent rates reflecting exemptions, assessment lag, and appeals, and where those mechanisms are significant a new buyer faces more, a wedge quantified in the companion methodology only for Oak Park (incumbent 2.44% versus a 3.40% statutory new-buyer rate from the Cook County Clerk’s 2024 Tax Rate Report). Income needed replicates MoneyLion’s method: 2 × (annual essential living expenses + annual mortgage cost), with a 10% down payment at the 6.47% 30-year fixed rate they used and a statewide living-expense constant of $11,043/yr backed out of their published Illinois income-needed figure against the June 2026 statewide ZHVI; the “+ tax” columns add effective rate × ZHVI to essential costs, which raises income needed by two dollars per tax dollar under their 50% essentials cap. County and village living costs are approximated by the statewide constant, which is conservative for high-cost areas. MoneyLion’s own payment examples (Maryland $2,461, Massachusetts $3,784) reproduce exactly as pure principal and interest (a third worked example, Hawaii, is omitted because its cited home value could not be re-verified against the archived study text), confirming the mortgage-payment component excludes property taxes and insurance. Green marks the best value in a column, red the worst; extremes are computed across counties only, excluding the statewide and Oak Park rows, whose geography class differs. Gap columns: green = income exceeds the threshold, red text = shortfall.
